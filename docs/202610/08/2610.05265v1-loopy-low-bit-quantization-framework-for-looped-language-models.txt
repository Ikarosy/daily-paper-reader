ABSTRACT
Looped language models provide a parameter-efficient way to scale iterative test-
time computation by repeatedly executing a shared recurrent core. Post-training
quantization (PTQ) can reduce the memory footprint and inference cost of looped
language models, but errors introduced by a quantized shared core affect sub-
sequent cores. Among PTQ methods, channel scaling and orthogonal rotations
preserve the floating-point computation while producing representations with dif-
ferent quantization quality.
We find that quantization configuration candidate
rankings can change with recurrent depth, motivating configuration selection at
the target deployment depth. However, evaluating every candidate over the full
calibration set at this depth is costly. We therefore propose LOOPY, a PTQ frame-
work that formulates shared-core quantization through a recurrent-depth-aware
objective, selecting shared low-bit representations by their final prediction loss at
the target deployment depth. Channel scaling and orthogonal rotations parame-
terize the candidate representations. To approximately solve this selection prob-
lem efficiently, LOOPY progressively allocates calibration windows to promis-
ing candidates while preserving complete target-depth execution, using only for-
ward evaluations. Across eight settings, LOOPY achieves the state-of-the-art re-
sults among different baselines. On Ouro-1.4B under W4A4, LOOPY reduces
LAMBADA perplexity by 36.5% relative to SpinQuant. Our code is available at
https://github.com/Shameless0817/Loopy-review.git.

INTRODUCTION
Looped language models increase effective depth by repeatedly executing a shared recurrent core
composed of transformer blocks, enabling iterative computation without storing a separate set of
weights for every recurrent step as shown in Figure 1 (Dehghani et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2025; Saun-
shi et al., 2025; Geiping et al., 2025). Post-training quantization (PTQ) provides a complementary
approach to efficient deployment by reducing the precision of weights and activations, thereby low-
ering memory requirements and enabling faster inference with low-bit kernels (Xiao et al., 2023;
Ashkboos et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). Applying PTQ to looped language models therefore offers
an effective solution to low-bit computation while preserving prediction quality.

Motivated by these observations, we introduce LOOPY, a recurrent-depth-aware PTQ framework for
looped language models, as shown in Figure 2. The key idea is to formulate shared-core quantization
as representation selection under a target-depth prediction objective: a shared quantized representa-
tion is evaluated by its final prediction loss after complete execution at the intended recurrent depth.
Channel scaling and orthogonal rotations define the candidate representation space. LOOPY approx-
imately solves the resulting selection problem through progressive full-depth evaluation, keeping the
recurrent depth fixed while allocating additional calibration windows to surviving candidates. This
procedure uses only forward evaluations, and the selected quantized weights and transformations
remain shared across recurrent steps.
We evaluate LOOPY on three representative looped language models: the Ouro series (Zhu et al.,
2025), Huginn (Geiping et al., 2025), and Recurrent-Llama (McLeish et al., 2025), under both
W4A8 and W4A4 quantization. Our main contributions are summarized as follows.
• We systematically study low-bit quantization in looped language models. We uncover
a loop-specific quantization phenomenon: a quantization configuration shared across re-
current steps can exhibit depth-dependent quality because of the recurrent dependency of
looped models. This serves as the motivation for our method LOOPY.
• We introduce LOOPY, a post-training quantization framework centered on a recurrent-
depth-aware objective for selecting shared low-bit representations. LOOPY parameterizes
these representations through channel scaling and orthogonal rotations, and approximately
solves the selection problem through progressive full-depth evaluation. This procedure pre-
serves the target recurrent depth while reducing calibration work by allocating additional
windows only to surviving candidates.
• We evaluate LOOPY across multiple looped language model families under W4A4 and
W4A8 quantization. On Ouro-1.4B under W4A4, LOOPY reduces LAMBADA perplexity
by 36.5% relative to the adapted SpinQuant baseline. We further evaluate LOOPY with
low-bit KV quantization and show that Q/K Hadamard transforms substantially mitigate
the degradation from 4-bit KV quantization.

4.2
MAIN RESULTS
W4A8. In Table 1, LOOPY achieves the lowest WikiText-2 and LAMBADA perplexity and the
highest available average accuracy among the evaluated low-bit methods on all four checkpoints.
W4A4. LOOPY achieves the lowest perplexity on both datasets for the two Ouro checkpoints and
Huginn-0125. Average accuracy exceeds the evaluated low-bit baselines on these three checkpoints.
Recurrent-Llama exhibits greater degradation under W4A4 than W4A8, compared SpinQuant under
W4A4, which is one of our work’s limitation. Its inherited Llama pre-normalization structure differs
from the normalization schemes used by Ouro and Huginn (McLeish et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025;
Geiping et al., 2025), potentially affecting the propagation of quantization perturbations through
recurrent execution.
Comparison with LoopQ. All LoopQ results are taken from its original paper (Fang et al., 2026),
while Loopy results are reused from Table 1. Table 2 shows that Loopy achieves lower W4A4
WikiText-2 perplexity and higher five-task average accuracy on both Ouro checkpoints.

4.4
KV CACHE QUANTIZATION
We evaluate 4-bit and 8-bit KV quantization on Ouro-1.4B at T = 4 with fixed Loopy W4A4 and
W4A8 configurations, optionally applying post-RoPE Q/K Hadamard transforms following Spin-
Quant (Liu et al., 2025). We report WikiText-2 perplexity and average accuracy over four down-
stream tasks, with the full evaluation protocol and per-task results provided in Appendix A.1.
As shown in Table 3, Q/K Hadamard transforms reduce the relative perplexity increase from KV4
quantization from 6.56% to 3.41% under W4A4 and from 4.38% to 2.19% under W4A8. They also
improve average accuracy by 1.40 percentage points over KV4 under W4A4.

RELATED WORK
Looped language models. Looped Transformers increase effective depth through repeated applica-
tion of shared parameters, as explored by Universal Transformers and subsequent looped language
models (Dehghani et al., 2019; Saunshi et al., 2025; Geiping et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025; Jeddi
et al., 2026). Parameter sharing reduces the number of distinct weights required to represent a deep
computation, motivating studies of memory-efficient recurrent architectures (Vendrell et al., 2026).
Post-training quantization. Existing methods reduce quantization error through weight reconstruc-
tion or channel scaling, as exemplified by GPTQ, AWQ, and SmoothQuant (Frantar et al., 2022; Lin
et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2023). Complementing these approaches, rotation-based methods redis-
tribute outliers, with QuaRot using structured orthogonal transformations and SpinQuant learning
rotations through an end-to-end quantization objective (Ashkboos et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025).
Recurrence-aware optimization. Repeated execution of the recurrent core produces evolving hid-
den states, allowing quantization errors to propagate across recurrent steps. LoopQ addresses this
problem through loop-aware transformations and trajectory-aware calibration (Fang et al., 2026).
6
CONCLUSION
We presented LOOPY, a PTQ framework that targets looped language models. By selecting shared
low-bit representations according to their final target-depth prediction loss, LOOPY aligns quanti-
zation selection with recurrent deployment and makes this selection practical through progressive
full-depth evaluation. Experiments show benefits varying across architectures.

A.1
KV QUANTIZATION PROTOCOL AND PER-TASK RESULTS
We evaluate ByteDance/Ouro-1.4B at T = 4, freezing the existing Loopy W4A4 and W4A8 weights
and transforms without additional search. The controls are BF16 KV, BF16 KV+H, KV8, KV4, and
KV4+H. Evaluation uses full-sequence teacher forcing with use cache=False. K and V are
quantized and dequantized for attention computation; these experiments assess prediction quality
rather than cached decoding performance.
Following SpinQuant (Liu et al., 2025), the +H variants apply the same normalized H128 to Q and
K after RoPE and before the attention dot product. K is quantized after this transform; Q receives
no additional quantization, and V receives no additional rotation. Otherwise, K is quantized directly
after RoPE. V is quantized after its projection and head partitioning. Both K and V use dynamic
asymmetric min–max quantization with a separate scale and zero-point for each token and head
(128 dimensions), including zero in the range and applying no clipping. KV8 and KV4 quantize both
tensors to 8 and 4 bits, respectively. Statistics and quantization arithmetic use FP32, and dequantized
outputs are cast to BF16.
WikiText-2 raw test text is concatenated and partitioned into non-overlapping blocks of up to 2048
tokens, retaining the final shorter block. The first token of each block is excluded from loss. We
aggregate NLL weighted by the number of valid prediction tokens and report PPL = exp(NLL),
over 149 blocks and 304,829 valid prediction tokens. This token-normalized protocol differs from
the word-normalized 4096-token protocol of the main results. Average accuracy is the unweighted
mean over ARC-Challenge, WinoGrande, LAMBADA, and HellaSwag, excluding MMLU.
Table 7 reports per-task accuracy. Under W4A4, Q/K Hadamard transforms improve all four task
accuracies relative to KV4, whereas under W4A8, task-level gains and losses largely offset each
other.

B
EVALUATION PROTOCOL
Model identity and scope. We evaluate four checkpoints at depths 4, 4, 32, and 16: Ouro-2.6B,
Ouro-1.4B, Huginn-0125, and Recurrent-Llama-3.2-1B. The Ouro sizes share a family. Checkpoint
names denote physical parameter count, not unrolled depth. The paper’s adapters quantize eligible
dense Transformer linears across the prelude, recurrent core, and coda, while Loopy transforms
only recurrent-core linears, retaining the AWQ anchor elsewhere. Embeddings, normalization, LM
heads, and early-exit gates remain unquantized.
Quantization recipes. LOOPY and AWQ both use symmetric group-wise W4 quantization with a
weight group size of 32, together with dynamic symmetric per-token A8 or A4 quantization. In the
W4A4 comparison in Table 1, our SmoothQuant adaptation uses group-wise W4 quantization with a
weight group size of 32 and dynamic A4 quantization, and our QuaRot adaptation (Ashkboos et al.,
2024) combines graph rotations with GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2022), our SpinQuant adaptation (Liu
et al., 2025) learns orthogonal transforms before GPTQ and uses symmetric A4. Our FlatQuant
adaptation (Sun et al., 2024) uses layerwise affine/diagonal transformations and clipping with W4
RTN. These recurrent-architecture adaptations use the same weight group size of 32. Activation
symmetry and calibration compute are not matched across methods. Collapsed adaptations do not
establish a failure of every implementation of the original method.
LoopQ comparison. Table 2 reuses the W4A4 Loopy results from Table 1 for Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-
2.6B at T = 4. LoopQ perplexities and task accuracies are taken from Table 1 of Fang et al. (2026);
its average accuracy is computed as the unweighted mean of HellaSwag, WinoGrande, LAMBADA,
ARC-Challenge, and MMLU. Quantization settings are aligned, while each method retains its own
calibration procedure.
Likelihood and downstream tasks. The full test splits are evaluated without a sample cap, with
batch size one. The main WikiText-2 results report word-normalized perplexity with a context length
of 4096 tokens, aligned with the LoopQ evaluation protocol. The calibration-depth ablation and
numerical-validation experiments use token-normalized perplexity on non-overlapping 2048-token
windows, excluding the first position of each window. LAMBADA perplexity and final-word accu-
racy follow the evaluator’s task definition. HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-Challenge, and MMLU
use zero-shot answer scoring. MMLU covers all 57 subjects. The paper runtime asserts the re-
quested number of recurrent steps and sets use cache=False. Huginn’s request-dependent state
initialization is seeded deterministically so that resumed shards use the same state.
B.1
SELECTION QUALITY AND CALIBRATION COST
The comparison uses Ouro-1.4B W4A4 at T = 4 and Huginn-0125 W4A8 at T = 32. Shallow uni-
form (T/2), full-depth uniform (T), shallow-to-full screening (T/4→T/2→T), and LOOPY share
the candidate pool, calibrated scaling bases, and quantization recipe within each model. Selection
regret is evaluated on the full selection set relative to the same-pool exhaustive optimum at target
depth. End-to-end calibration time includes base calibration, candidate construction or restoration,
screening, and final model construction, and is normalized by exhaustive search. Figure 6 presents
the selection quality–cost comparison.
B.2
CALIBRATION DEPTH AT FIXED DEPLOYMENT DEPTH
The following ablations retain the original token-normalized WikiText-2 perplexity protocol with
2048-token windows.
We vary calibration depth while fixing deployment depth for each model. Ouro-2.6B W4A8 and
Ouro-1.4B W4A4 use Tcal ∈{1, 2, 4} and Tdeploy = 4; Huginn-0125 W4A8 uses Tcal ∈{8, 16, 32}
and Tdeploy = 32. Within each configuration, the candidate pool and selection windows remain
fixed. We reselect candidates by rollout NLL at each calibration depth, and evaluate all selected

H20 DECODING THROUGHPUT
Evaluation protocol. We benchmark Ouro-1.4B at T = 4 on a NVIDIA H20 GPU, using batch
size/input length pairs of 1/128 and 4/2048. Each run generates 128 tokens without EOS stopping,
with the first token produced during prefill and the remaining 127 through cached decoding. Aggre-
gate batch throughput is 127B/tdecode, measured with CUDA-synchronized wall-clock timing over
the complete decoding loop, including Python scheduling but excluding prefill and initialization.
All methods use fresh BF16 KV caches with 96 recurrent slots, Flash SDPA, and eager execution
without CUDA Graphs or profiling. We report the arithmetic mean of ten throughput measurements,
collected in two blocks of three warm-up runs followed by five fixed-input measurements.
Offline and hybrid configurations. Loopy (offline) combines Q/K/V- and gate/up-shared input
scaling absorbed into RMSNorm and linear weights, head-wise V–O Hadamard rotation with block
size 128 absorbed into paired weights, and up–down scaling that does not cross the SiLU gate.
These transforms are shared across recurrent calls and require no explicit online transformation,
while activation quantization remains dynamic. This constrained configuration differs from the
online Loopy checkpoint. The two hybrid configurations add down-input or down- and Q/K/V-
input rotations using seed 42 and block size 128 with matching weights. All three configurations
reuse compatible input quantization results across Q/K/V and gate/up without fusing their matrix
multiplications.
Kernel differences and diagnostic entries. Loopy uses group-128 Triton packed-W4 kernels,
whereas the adapted QuaRot and SpinQuant kernels use per-output-channel weight scales, with
additional asymmetric activation zero-point correction for SpinQuant. Attention and timing scope
are shared, so the results compare implementations rather than isolate transformation overhead. En-
tries marked ∗denote W4A4 integer paths whose NLL differences from BF16 evaluation exceed
the predefined tolerance; their throughput cannot be paired directly with BF16-path quality results.
W4A8 passes this check on four calibration windows, but full quality evaluation was not repeated
on the timed execution paths.
